# mobile-eeg-artifact-benchmark

Code and per-participant results for:

> A decoding-centred benchmark of IMU-referenced motion-artifact removal for ambulatory EEG-BCI, with a causal motion-gated replay. Yao Xie and Lirui Sun, 2026 (manuscript under review).

Everything in the paper's tables is regenerated by `code/make_tables.py` from the JSON files in `results/`. The scripts that produce those JSON files are listed below; they need the public datasets, which are **not** redistributed here.

## Participants and the two releases (read this first)

The Mobile BCI dataset exists in two public releases. The figshare release (article 13604078) holds 18 participants as 500 Hz raw `.mat` files (`s01`–`s18`); the OSF release (project r7s9b) holds all 24 participants as 100 Hz BrainVision/BIDS files (`sub-01`–`sub-24`) with bad-channel annotations and integer-quantized IMU channels. **The numbering differs between the releases.** Matching the stimulus-onset sequences of every recording (`code/match_osf_figshare.py`; result in `results/osf_figshare_mapping.json`) shows that figshare `s01`–`s18` are OSF `sub-11, sub-12, sub-05, sub-13, sub-14, sub-06, sub-19, sub-07, sub-08, sub-17, sub-09, sub-10, sub-20, sub-18, sub-21, sub-22, sub-23, sub-24` respectively, and that the six OSF participants absent from figshare are **`sub-01, sub-02, sub-03, sub-04, sub-15, sub-16`**.

The analysis therefore uses figshare `s01`–`s18` plus OSF `sub-01/02/03/04/15/16` (24 distinct participants). An earlier draft of this work mistakenly used OSF `sub-19`–`sub-24` as the "additional" participants; those are duplicates of figshare `s07, s13, s15, s16, s17, s18`. Results computed with the duplicates are kept in `results/*/_v3_with_duplicates/` for the record and for the cross-release concordance analysis (`code/release_concordance.py`, Supplement S16).

## Environment

Standard-order ASR (`asr_std`, `asr10_std`) lives in `code/p1_asr_standard.py`.

## What is in `results/`

- `p1_clean_eval/*.json` — per-participant decoding performance (`per_subject`) and condition means (`summary`) for every retrospective run; `heldout_param_selection.json`, `timing.json`, `imu_only_decoding.json`, `ssvep_signal_probe.json`.
- `p1_causal_replay/results_thr{50,100,200}_mu0.05.json`, `results_thr100_mu0.05_hp0.1.json` — causal replay per participant, per-second gate decisions per segment, transition metrics; `heldout_gate_threshold.json`.
- `tables_v3.md` / `tables_v3.json` — every table in the paper and supplement, generated from the above (Hodges–Lehmann shifts included).
- `osf_figshare_mapping.json`, `figshare_event_onsets_100hz.json`, `release_concordance.json` — the release mapping and the concordance analysis.
- `*/_v3_with_duplicates/` — result files of the earlier draft that double-counted six participants (not used by the paper).
- `mobile_bci_baseline/`, `wrist_exo_baseline/`, `wrist_encoder_reference/` — original-protocol baselines and the Supplement S1 experiment.

## Notes that matter for reproduction

- The two releases differ: figshare is 500 Hz raw (IMU in physical units); OSF is 100 Hz with bad-channel annotations and integer-quantized IMU channels (standing yields constant values). The causal gate's calibration denominator is floored at 1e-30 for this reason.
- ASR is applied in the standard order (original reference, bad channels removed, before interpolation and CAR). Applying CAR and interpolation first makes the EEG covariance rank-deficient and meegkit's ASR diverges on OSF participants with interpolated channels; the `asr`/`asr10` methods in `p1_clean_eval.py` keep the earlier rank-safe workaround for the sensitivity analysis.
- Held-out folds use seed 20261004 (`heldout_param_selection.py`, `heldout_gate_threshold.py`); fold identities are stored in their outputs and change with the participant list.
- The OSF files API throttles parallel crawls (HTTP 429); `osf_gentle_list.py` lists sequentially with back-off.
